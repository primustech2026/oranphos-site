Batch-document guide

Reading an MKP COA, parameter by parameter

Eight rows become useful only after you separate the specification ceiling from the measured batch value.

5 min read

A buyer receives an MKP COA with 8 analytical rows and does not know which one should stop a 25 t order. Assay reads 99.2%, water insolubles read 0.07%, chloride reads 0.0014% and every value appears to pass. The document becomes useful only after each result is linked to a method, an internal limit and the intended application.

What does an MKP COA specification mean?

An MKP COA reports measured results for 1 identified production lot, while the specification states acceptable minimums or maximums. Read assay, P₂O₅, K₂O, insolubles, chloride, moisture, pH and heavy metals together; then compare 10 consecutive lots, because 1 passing certificate cannot show process variation.

The first check is identity and traceability, not chemistry. Confirm product name, lot number, production date, test date, method references, authorized signature and whether the lot matches the packing list. A document titled “Certificate of Analysis” without a batch number is functionally a specification sheet, because no 25 kg bag or 1,000-bag container can be traced back to it.

Why are assay, P₂O₅ and K₂O separate?

Assay estimates KH₂PO₄ purity, while P₂O₅ and K₂O express the nutrient elements in conventional fertilizer units. The 0-52-34 declaration is not the literal molecular composition printed as P and K; it is an oxide-equivalent convention. A credible lot should show a coherent relationship among all 3 values rather than 3 unrelated numbers copied from a brochure.

Small method and rounding differences are normal, but a large inconsistency deserves a calculation and retest. Ask whether results are reported on an as-is or dry basis and which edition of the method was used. Comparing a dry-basis 52.2% P₂O₅ result with an as-is internal limit can create a false dispute if moisture treatment is not aligned.

How should water insolubles be translated into risk?

Percentages hide the mass introduced during a normal batching shift. At 300 kg MKP input, 0.07% water insolubles equals 300 kg × 0.0007 = 0.210 kg, or 210 g. At a 0.10% ceiling, the same 300 kg equals 300 g, so the lower value represents 90 g less residue and a 30% reduction against the 300 g reference.

The calculation does not predict particle size or guarantee a clean 0.8 mm dripper. Hard water, calcium contamination, cold recrystallization and poor filtration can create deposits after the bag is opened. Use insolubles as the incoming product load, then validate the complete B-tank recipe and filter system under the intended 20°C or minimum-night conditions.

What does a low chloride result tell you?

A chloride result such as 0.0014% is useful only when the method detection and reporting basis are stated. Chloride-sensitive crops and foliar products may require tighter internal limits than bulk soil fertilizer. Compare 10 lots, because 1 unusually low value does not establish whether the acid and alkali inputs are controlled routinely.

Moisture and pH provide additional consistency signals. Moisture affects flow and caking in 25 kg PE-lined bags, while pH should be measured at a stated MKP concentration and temperature. A pH value without “1% solution at 25°C,” or another defined condition, cannot be compared cleanly with a second laboratory’s result.

How do specification ceilings differ from measured values?

A line reading “water insolubles ≤0.10%” is the acceptance ceiling; a result of 0.07% is the measured value for that lot. Procurement should store both fields separately, because replacing every result with the limit destroys trend information. The current MKP specification and typical lot values show how the guarantee and the operating result should be presented side by side.

Use a 10-lot table with minimum, maximum, average and any retest flag. A stable series between 0.05% and 0.08% provides more process evidence than 10 rows copied as “≤0.10%.” If the supplier refuses original lot values and offers only ceilings, treat the file as marketing data and keep the source unapproved.

What should happen before approval?

  1. Match the COA lot number to the 25 kg bag mark, packing list and sample seal.
  2. Confirm methods and reporting basis for all 8 parameters.
  3. Compare 10 consecutive lots against internal limits and calculate variation.
  4. Test a DHL sample internally and retain part of the same lot for 6 months.
  5. Use SGS sampling and loading supervision for the first 25 t container.

Key Takeaways

  • A COA belongs to 1 lot; a file without a batch number is not batch evidence.
  • Store the specification limit and measured value in separate fields.
  • At 300 kg input, 0.07% insolubles equals 210 g and 0.10% equals 300 g.
  • Compare 10 lots and verify the first 25 t container through SGS.
