Fertigation field guide

MKP fertigation programs for greenhouse tomato

Five growth stages, one B-tank rule and rates that can be checked against EC before they reach the root zone.

8 min read · Target keyword: MKP fertigation program tomato

A greenhouse manager crouches beside the B tank at the season change, reducing the dosing pump from 1.2% while the first tomato trusses begin to set. Last week’s vegetative recipe carried more nitrate; this week the crop needs a deliberate rise in phosphorus and potassium without a sudden EC jump. The practical question is not whether MKP contains 0-52-34, but how many grams enter each 1,000 L of irrigation water at each stage.

What is an MKP fertigation program for tomato?

A tomato MKP program normally moves from 150–250 g/1,000 L after transplanting to 600–800 g/1,000 L during fruit coloring. Keep MKP in the B tank, separate from calcium nitrate concentrate, and confirm every stage against source-water EC, drain EC and crop response before raising the injection rate.

The table below is a working framework, not a fixed prescription for every 10,000 m² greenhouse. Cultivar, substrate, radiation, irrigation frequency, source-water bicarbonate and the rest of the formula change the final dose. Use the MKP number to place P and K in the program, then calculate nitrogen, calcium, magnesium and micronutrients around the complete analysis rather than copying one row into the injector.

Why does MKP stay in the B tank?

MKP and calcium nitrate should never share the same concentrated stock tank, because phosphate and calcium can form calcium-phosphate precipitate before the injector provides 100-fold or 200-fold dilution. The white material may settle in the tank, load a disc filter or pass as fine particles toward 0.8 mm emitters. Keep calcium nitrate in A and MKP, magnesium sulfate and compatible chelated trace elements in B, following the detailed A/B tank compatibility guide.

Dilution changes the ion activity, so 2 materials that are unsafe at 10% stock concentration may coexist after injection into the main line. That does not justify connecting both concentrates to one suction manifold or leaving a mixing valve open between A and B. Use separate pumps, non-return valves and clearly labeled transfer hoses; 1 maintenance shortcut can undo a full season of correct chemistry.

How should the dose be introduced?

  1. Test source-water pH, alkalinity and EC before setting the first 150 g/1,000 L rate.
  2. Dissolve MKP in clean water, keeping a 100 L B-tank concentrate below the cold-night solubility margin.
  3. Inject at the low end for 2–3 irrigations and record supply EC, drain EC and drain percentage.
  4. Increase in 50–100 g/1,000 L steps only when plant load and drain data support the change.
  5. Run a 1 L jar test whenever water source, micronutrient blend or stock concentration changes.

For a 1,000 L working solution, 500 g of MKP contributes about 260 g P₂O₅ and 170 g K₂O before the rest of the fertilizer recipe is counted. That calculation prevents double-loading K when potassium nitrate or potassium sulfate is already supplying the same nutrient. It also makes the cost comparison honest, because price per tonne matters less than delivered kilograms of P₂O₅ and K₂O in the final formula.

Where does EC management enter?

MKP is only one part of total EC, and a 700 g/1,000 L dose should not be evaluated in isolation from calcium nitrate, magnesium sulfate, potassium nitrate and source-water salts. Record the EC after all fertilizers are injected, then compare root-zone or drain EC at the same time each day. If drain EC climbs for 3 consecutive readings, review irrigation volume and total salt load before assuming the tomato needs more K.

Hot, high-radiation days can support more water flow while cold, low-radiation days may need less total nutrient delivery even when the stock recipe stays unchanged. A dosing system that injects by percentage will deliver more kilograms when irrigation volume rises, so daily MKP mass should be logged alongside g/1,000 L. A 20% increase in water volume is also a 20% increase in MKP mass if injection concentration is unchanged.

What should be checked in the product?

For drip use, review assay, water insolubles, chloride, moisture and pH on each MKP COA. At 300 kg input, 0.07% water insolubles equals 210 g, while a 0.10% ceiling equals 300 g; that is 90 g less theoretical residue presented to the system. The greenhouse-grade MKP specification shows the batch parameters that should be confirmed before a 25 t order.

Key Takeaways

  • Move from 150–250 g/1,000 L after transplanting toward 600–800 g/1,000 L at coloring only as crop load and EC data support it.
  • Keep MKP in B and calcium nitrate in A; concentrated phosphate and calcium are an absolute no-mix pair.
  • Count MKP together with every other K source, including potassium nitrate and potassium sulfate.
  • Log daily kilograms as well as g/1,000 L because irrigation volume changes total nutrient delivery.
