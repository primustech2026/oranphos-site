How to qualify a second MKP source: 5-step checklist

A backup source should be qualified before a 15% increase, missed vessel or crop-season deadline forces the decision.

8 min read

A purchasing manager opens an email announcing a 15% MKP price increase effective on the next 25 t container. The current source has performed acceptably for 4 years, but no approved alternative exists in the ERP. That is not the moment to begin laboratory work; it is the moment the cost of having skipped qualification becomes visible.

How do you qualify a second MKP source?

Qualify a second source in 5 gates: review 10 consecutive lot COAs, test a DHL sample internally, compare both sources with one template, supervise a 25 t trial container through SGS, then require 3 stable production batches before monthly allocation. Keep every result linked to lot and shipment numbers.

The objective is not to prove that a new source can produce 1 good sample. It is to determine whether routine MKP production, packing and export documents stay inside your limits across time. A 5-gate process prevents sales urgency from collapsing technical approval into a single COA and prevents laboratory approval from ignoring container loading, moisture protection or B/L accuracy.

  1. Request 10 lot COAs, not a specification sheet

    A specification sheet states ceilings and floors; 10 consecutive COAs show the operating distribution. Build a row for assay, P₂O₅, K₂O, water insolubles, chloride, moisture, pH and the relevant heavy-metal panel, then record minimum, maximum and average. Reject a pack that removes batch numbers or selects nonconsecutive “best” lots, because it cannot demonstrate normal variance.

    Compare measured values with your internal acceptance limits, not only the supplier’s limits. If your fertigation program requires water insolubles at or below 0.10%, a series ranging from 0.05% to 0.08% gives more room than 10 lots parked at 0.10%. The MKP lot parameters and packing basis provide a starting set of rows for the comparison template.

  2. Run a DHL sample through your own system

    Ask for a 500 g to 1 kg sealed sample carrying the same lot number as its COA. Test identity, appearance, dissolution, pH, moisture and the parameters your application can verify; then run a practical B-tank check using the actual source water. A supplier laboratory result and your internal result should be close enough to explain within method uncertainty.

    Keep part of the DHL sample as a reference for at least 6 months or through the trial-container decision. Photograph the seal, label and condition on arrival, because sample integrity is part of traceability. Do not approve a different production lot simply because the first sample looked clear in a 1 L beaker.

  3. Use one side-by-side comparison template

    Place the incumbent and candidate in the same 1-page template: measured chemistry, 20°C dissolution observation, bag construction, pallet plan, lead time, Incoterm, payment, document set and complaint response. Normalize freight and terms so FOB Shanghai, FOB Qingdao and CIF offers are not compared as if they contain the same cost. Add 30/70 T/T against B/L copy to the cash-flow row when that is the offered structure.

    Score critical items as pass/fail before assigning any weighted price score. A missing lot number on the COA is not compensated by USD 15/t savings, and a bag that fails humidity protection is not corrected by a 2-day faster production promise. The template should make those boundaries visible to technical, finance and procurement in the same review.

  4. Place 1 SGS-supervised trial container

    Use 1 FCL—normally 1,000 bags × 25 kg—for the first commercial validation. Ask SGS or another agreed inspector to verify bag count, marks, container condition, sampling and seal number, then retain a composite sample tied to the inspection report. Match the production COA, SGS result, packing list and B/L to the same lot or clearly documented blended-lot structure.

    Receive the container as a controlled trial rather than mixing it invisibly into monthly stock. Inspect 20 bags across pallet positions for damage, caking, print consistency and net weight, and record warehouse behavior for 30 days. Release to customers only through the approval path used for any new raw material.

  5. Require 3 stable batches before monthly allocation

    One container proves that 1 shipment can work; 3 batches begin to show repeatability. Compare the first 3 COAs, internal checks, packing observations, lead times and document corrections, then approve a limited monthly share such as 20–30% rather than moving 100% immediately. Increase allocation only after the candidate performs through routine production pressure and at least 1 repeat order.

    Define the review trigger before approval: for example, water insolubles above 0.10%, chloride above the internal limit, a missed 14-day lead time or 2 document errors in 3 shipments. A written trigger removes emotion from the next decision. It also tells the new source exactly which failures will pause allocation.

What is the commercial value if you never switch?

A validated alternative has value even when the incumbent keeps 100% of volume. Current 10-lot data, an approved sample and a known 25 t landed cost give procurement a credible reference during annual negotiation. The cheapest bargaining tool is not a threatening email; it is a second source that has already passed technical gates.

The exercise also exposes weaknesses in the incumbent record. If the candidate can provide batch-linked COAs, GHS SDS, certificate copies and an SGS loading protocol within 24 hours, those items become reasonable expectations for the current source. Qualification raises the minimum documentation standard for both suppliers without requiring an immediate purchase switch.

Key Takeaways

  • Start with 10 consecutive COAs; a specification sheet cannot show routine variance.
  • Keep the DHL sample lot, internal result and supplier COA tied together for 6 months.
  • Use SGS on the first 25 t container and inspect at least 20 bags across pallet positions.
  • Require 3 stable batches before moving a candidate into monthly allocation.
  • Even without switching, a validated alternative is the lowest-cost improvement to your negotiating position.
