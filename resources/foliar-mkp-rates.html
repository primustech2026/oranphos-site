Foliar MKP: concentrations by crop and growth stage

Rates are written as percentages, but the spray operator needs kilograms per 100 L and a clear stop point.

5 min read

A grower stands beside a 500 L sprayer and asks whether to add 2.5 kg or 10 kg of MKP before the evening pass. Both numbers can appear in published programs, but they represent 0.5% and 2.0% solutions with very different crop and temperature risks. The safe answer starts with crop type, leaf age, growth stage and a small untreated comparison strip.

What is a practical MKP foliar spray concentration rate?

Use 0.5–1.0% MKP on vegetables, 1.0–2.0% on established fruit-tree foliage and 1.5–2.0% on tolerant field crops, starting at the low end. Spray below 28°C in early morning or evening, keep calcium products out, and treat 2.5% as a leaf-burn warning threshold—not a target.

A 1.0% solution means 1 kg MKP in enough water to make 100 L final spray, not 1 kg added after the tank is already full. For a 500 L sprayer, 0.5%, 1.0% and 2.0% equal 2.5 kg, 5 kg and 10 kg respectively. Agitation, water volume per hectare and crop canopy determine the kilograms applied to 1 hectare, so concentration alone is incomplete.

Why does timing change the burn risk?

Foliar salts concentrate as water evaporates, so a 2.0% spray applied at 34°C can become a much stronger deposit on the leaf surface within minutes. Early morning and evening applications slow evaporation and extend wetting time, but leaves should still dry before a prolonged humid night creates disease pressure. A practical operating window is below 28°C with moderate wind and no rainfall expected for 4–6 hours.

Young vegetable leaves usually tolerate less salt than hardened fruit-tree leaves, which is why 0.5% is a sensible first screen. The leaf-burn risk rises above 2.5%, especially when MKP is combined with other soluble fertilizers or an aggressive adjuvant. Do not treat 2.5% as a universal failure point; treat it as a clear reason to stop and obtain crop-specific evidence.

How should a foliar tank be mixed?

  1. Fill the clean spray tank to 30–50% with water and start agitation.
  2. Dissolve the required MKP separately; 5 kg makes a 1.0% final concentration in 500 L.
  3. Add the MKP solution through the tank screen, then add compatible labeled products one at a time.
  4. Top up to 500 L, confirm pH and inspect for haze, sediment, heat or crystal formation.
  5. Spray a 20–50 plant strip and inspect after 24 and 48 hours before treating the full block.

Calcium nitrate, calcium chloride and other calcium sources are prohibited partners for concentrated MKP because calcium phosphate can precipitate. The risk does not disappear merely because the spray tank is more dilute than a B tank. If a crop program needs both foliar P-K and calcium, use separate applications with a suitable interval based on the product labels and local agronomic advice.

Hard water can add calcium, magnesium and bicarbonate before the first fertilizer is measured. A 1 L jar test should therefore use the actual spray water and the full planned concentration, including adjuvant. Inspect immediately and again after 2 hours; a clear mix that deposits crystals as temperature drops from 25°C to 10°C is not ready for a long field shift.

How do you calculate field application?

Concentration and spray volume must be multiplied. A 1.0% MKP solution applied at 500 L/ha delivers 5 kg MKP/ha, while the same 1.0% at 1,000 L/ha delivers 10 kg/ha. The second program doubles the hectare dose even though the tank percentage is identical, so both values belong on the spray order.

Coverage also changes with nozzle, pressure and canopy. A fine droplet may improve surface coverage but increase drift, while heavy runoff wastes the 5 kg/ha dose and may concentrate salts at leaf edges. Calibrate the sprayer with water over a measured 1 ha or smaller test area before calculating the MKP load.

What product checks matter for foliar use?

Review water insolubles and chloride as well as assay. Fine insoluble material can load nozzle screens, while unnecessary chloride is undesirable in a low-chloride foliar program. The MKP 0-52-34 lot profile lists 0.07% typical water insolubles and 0.0014% chloride, but the actual COA for the shipped lot should control release.

A 25 kg bag also needs dry, free-flowing condition so the operator can weigh 2.5–10 kg accurately without breaking hard lumps into the tank. Seal the remainder against humidity and record the batch number on the spray sheet. If leaf response differs between 2 passes, the lot record helps separate product, weather, water and application variables.

Key Takeaways

  • Start at 0.5–1.0% for vegetables, 1.0–2.0% for fruit trees and 1.5–2.0% for field crops.
  • A 1.0% mix equals 5 kg MKP in a 500 L final spray volume.
  • Spray below 28°C and treat concentrations above 2.5% as a leaf-burn warning.
  • Keep calcium products out and test 20–50 plants for 48 hours before a full block.
